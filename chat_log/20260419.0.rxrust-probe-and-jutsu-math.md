## Goal

Extend `throughput_probe` with rxrust composition-layer variants to answer:
does wrapping Hopp2 topology through rxrust Observable/Scheduler primitives
cost measurable throughput? Then lift the probe toward a 3-axis matrix
(stack × arrival × effect × policy) so sprf's eventual parse-into-sqlite
engine can use the same harness to validate its effect pipeline.

Side thread: audit `/async-jutsu` and `/batching-jutsu` against what the
probe actually proved, amend with math the taxonomy assumed but did not
derive, add measurement-hygiene sections.

  1. Per-item amortization: `t(N) = F + Nv`, `per_item = F/N + v`

## Experimental findings

### Rxrust composition tax

Under the sprefa target regime (real ast-grep scan work), rxrust
is within noise of raw crossbeam. At 500 items × --work-dist real,
all four variants (Cdam2, Rxrust1, Hopp2:W=1, Rxrust1Buf) produce
identical (files, bytes, matches) = (500, 2202170, 242) and wall
times within 1ms at 163-165ms.

At zero-work + zero-cost microbench (41k items, 20 trials):

    variant             wall_p50    batch_p50    notes
    Rxrust1Buf:2048:64    1.20 ms         64     bimodal: p10=1.15, p90=2.03
    Cdam2Buf:2048:64      2.56 ms         64     hand-rolled matched
    Hopp2:2048:64:1       2.66 ms          3     opportunistic drains
                                                 before producer fills
    Rxrust1:2048          3.93 ms          1     1-at-a-time rxrust
    Cdam2:2048            4.01 ms          1     raw 1-at-a-time

Two surprises:

1. Rxrust1 ties with Cdam2 at 41k items; earlier 12% tax at 3
   trials was trial-to-trial noise. Proper 10-20 trial runs show
   within-noise.
2. Rxrust1Buf beats hand-written Cdam2Buf by 2× at zero-work
   under matched batching model. Unexplained; likely compiler
   monomorphization through rxrust observer chain inlines to a
   tighter inner loop than `if batch.len() >= max_batch` branch.
   Passthrough-is-measured-not-axiomatic added to skill because
   of this.

Under any paced producer (`fixed:5us`) or real per-item work
(`fixed:10us`), all variants converge within 0.5%.

### Earlier 2× Rxrust1Buf vs Hopp2 W=1 "win" was artifact

Hopp2 W=1 opportunistic drain yielded `batch_p50=3` at zero-work
(consumer outpaces producer, drain always empty). Rxrust1Buf
forced `batch_p50=64`. ~21× more dispatch calls for Hopp2 is NOT
a topology delta, it's a **batching-model asymmetry**. Exposed by
adding Cdam2Buf (same batching model as Rxrust1Buf, raw stack).

### Tier A — real effect kinds (single commit)

Add `EffectCost` trait with three implementations matching the
batching-jutsu cost-model table:

- `GitOdbRead` — mock syscall + Mutex fixed cost, flat variable.
  10-100µs fixed, ~0 variable. LARGE amortization.
- `SqliteInsertTx` — real `:memory:` rusqlite + `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`
  + N inserts + `COMMIT`. ~1ms fixed (commit fsync sim), row
  variable. LARGE stepped — the only case windowed-trailing
  beats opportunistic.
- `RayonAstGrep` — already-parallel baseline via rayon par_iter on
  the full batch. Shows batching-on-top throughput ceiling = 1/C.

Wire dispatch through `--effect SqliteInsertTx:tx_size=64` etc.
Expect:
- SqliteInsertTx + Opportunistic: loses to SqliteInsertTx + WindowedTrailing
  because stepped fixed cost is the one regime where waiting
  amortizes future arrivals.
- RayonAstGrep + any batching: loses to RayonAstGrep + Passthrough
  because wrapping par_iter serializes it.
- GitOdbRead + Opportunistic ≈ GitOdbRead + WindowedLeading
  at high arrival rate (flat amortization curve).

This closes the "does rxrust cost us when the effect is
group-commit sqlite?" question that was the original driver of
this thread.
