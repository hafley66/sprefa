GRAPH-LIB LANDSCAPE (web-verified): static math is portable for free — petgraph
(tarjan_scc, condensation, toposort, reachability) covers scc.rs; datafrog/ascent/
crepe = portable batch datalog fixpoint. INCREMENTAL+RETRACTION has NO clean
portable non-DD lib: only differential-dataflow (= the rejected runtime; FlowLog
VLDB2026 compiles datalog->DD), or salsa (memoization shape, not relational
retraction). That gap is WHY v5 hand-rolls retraction. Realistic portable core =
petgraph/datafrog for pure math behind RelStore seam + v5's own sync-tick
retraction.

DD PEAK-RSS (web-verified): footprint dominated by arrangement TRACES; structural
~4x amplification (cited: 400MB logical -> 1.6GB peak). Levers: (1)
set_logical_compaction(frontier) every tick (compact to current snapshot, no
time-travel) = biggest knob; (2) set_physical_compaction (CPU for RSS); (3)
consolidate() to cancel +1/-1; (4) merge-spine causes transient ~2x spike = what
peak_rss actually catches; (5) one worker/active-repo. THE WALL: SQLite SPILLS to
disk (RSS bounded by cache_size pragma, dataset-independent); DD is in-memory so
RSS floor = compacted working set × amplification + spike. Self-defeat: bounding DD
to "current snapshot every tick" discards the very thing DD is for. CONCLUSION:
bounded peak RSS at 500-repo scale -> DD is the WRONG tool (SQLite spill wins); DD
only earns RSS on the small active-repo LSP set.

## Key Insights
- The engine was never the gap — product surface was. v5 already answers Glean's
  canonical questions from real code; the work is ergonomics + a couple decisions.
- `where` was pure sugar EXCEPT closure point-queries (rule bodies can't read
  closures). The fix wasn't to keep `where` — it was to make closures SEEDABLE FROM
  NESTING (one endpoint pinned to a literal -> seeded BFS over the cond cache).
  Now nesting is the one canonical filter; literal head term seeds a closure.
- No library bundles graph-algos + datalog + incremental-retraction in a portable
  non-DD shape. Static math is free (petgraph/datafrog). The retraction reactivity
  is the only un-libraried part — that's the moat AND why v5 welds it to SQLite.
- DD's value (incremental over a history of times) is exactly what you must throw
  away to bound its peak RSS; at 500-repo scale SQLite's disk-spill wins outright.
  DD only fits the small active-repo LSP set.
- Chris's thesis: datalog solve = commodity; moat = incremental priming + measuring
  per-iteration cost so users don't pre-declare loop cost ("we do it for them").
  v5 already measures per-TICK; the gap is per-RULE per-ITERATION attribution +
  persistence. That measurement is what lets streaming work without the DD runtime.
- ghcache reframes 500-repo: it owns polling (gh->SQLite+checkouts); sprefa watches
  change_log. Don't build batch re-enumeration perf.
