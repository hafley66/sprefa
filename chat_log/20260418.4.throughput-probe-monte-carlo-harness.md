## Problem/Context
Original session 1 produced findings that topology (Cdam/Gopp/Hopp) clustered within 10% — those numbers turned out to be noise because scan was on the producer (rayon par_iter), making all consumer-side work <1% of wall time. Moved scan to consumer in v2 variants; topology differences became measurable.

Second iteration added Pareto work-distribution sampling + MC seed-sweep aggregation. Three audit agents (2 Sonnet, 1 Opus) then found critical issues: xorshift trap state on rng=0, percentile estimator biased at small n, thermal confound from sequential seed order, Pareto α=1.1 harsher than real file distributions, probe missing variant_b-shape baseline.

## Key Insights
1. **Single-seed runs lie by up to 7× under heavy-tailed per-item cost.** Fixed-cost control shows <1.4% spread; Pareto(α=1.1) shows 140-688% spread. Any single-point measurement under heavy tail is noise.
2. **Experimental design determines what's measurable.** Producer-side-scan variants can never distinguish consumer topology — the scan dominates. Had to move scan to consumer to see channel discipline effects at all.
3. **More workers help average throughput but inflate tail variance under heavy tail.** W=16 p90 may be worse than W=4 p90 because max-of-N scaling on Pareto tails — but this specific claim NEEDS more trials to validate.
4. **The "default MAX_BATCH=32" rule is workload-dependent.** Under constant cost, B=32 captures 98% amortization. Under Pareto tail, small B insulates dispatch p95 from being dominated by the one tail sample in the batch. Two different optimum regimes, one knob.
5. **`thread::sleep` is an inflated model of fixed cost.** Real syscalls/locks keep the CPU hot; sleep yields and the cache goes cold on resume. Batching amortization findings are upper bounds — real crossover where batching pays is at higher fixed costs than this probe suggests.
6. **Cross-sonnet audits catch what single-session work misses.** BLOCKER bug (rng=0 trap state), statistical invalidity of small-n Pareto percentile claims, thermal confound in sequential bash loops — none of these would have surfaced without independent review.

## Open Questions
- What IS the actual file-size distribution in swc / linux kernel? Need to fit empirical data before picking a realistic Pareto shape. Log-normal may be the better fit.
- Does the Hopp2:8 sweet spot generalize to 32-core Linux, or is it the "saturate cores you have" tautology that Opus called out?
- How does the probe behave under page-cache-cold conditions? Every current number is page-hot via the `_warm` loop.
- Does backpressure on a downstream stage (simulated writer/reporter queue) change the W sweet spot? Currently consumers just fold to atomics.
- For `--fixed-cost-dist` modes (Pareto/Normal/Exp), is a macOS `thread::sleep` with <100µs jitter even a useful model, or should the probe busy-loop the fixed cost via `std::hint::spin_loop` inside a deadline check?
- Would adding variant_b2 (producer-side scan via rayon WalkParallel, matching ripgrep) show that topology doesn't matter at all and the whole Hopp2-vs-Cdam2 sweep is an artifact of the consumer-side-scan choice?
