## 2. The measured verdict (from PERF-REPORT.md)

- **All correct engines produce byte-identical output** across the whole matrix
  (hashes match, verified cross-process). Inputs verified identical too.
- **Counting is fast and correct on DAGs, WRONG on cycles** (it keeps phantom-cycle
  rows alive — flagged `NO` at CYC scales). ~0.44s @ 960k nodes, 29 stmts.
- **DRed is correct on cycles but ~5x slower than counting** (~2.05s @ 960k, 75
  stmts). Its cost is the two passes (over-delete the whole reachable cone, then
  rederive), not sqlite abuse — every statement is delta-proportional, PK-driven.
- **The CTE form is ~20% SLOWER than the loop** (2.5s vs 2.05s @ 960k), despite 6
  statements vs 75. SQLite's recursive CTE materializes a working table without the
  index-driven temp-table frontier the hand loop uses. **CTE-is-faster was a wrong
  assumption; measured and rejected.** (Known lab result, now reproduced here.)
- **dd is fastest (~0.17s @ 960k) but resident**: `rust_live` scales ~215 B/node
  (12→47→187→618 MB at 60k→240k→960k→2.9M). The store's `rust_live` is **0.09 MB
  flat** — state on disk. That is the whole tradeoff, and the breakpoint ramp forces
  dd's abort while the store keeps going.
- **SCC counting landed.** `retract_scc` keeps the counting pass for cross-component
  deltas, then persists the affected cone and runs a PK-frontier nested least
  fixpoint from surviving external support. At CYC 960k it produced the oracle's
  815,240 keys in 2.89s / 56 harness statements, versus counting's incorrect
  830,478 in 0.41s / 29 and DRed-loop's 815,240 in 2.36s / 75. Its SQLite
  high-water was 98.69 MB and Rust peak 0.16 MB; the latter stays low because
  the scope and frontier remain SQLite tables.

## 4. Algorithmic gaps for tomorrow

**G1 — Port SqliteTemporal to the store (temporal fact plane).**
The one real sqlite finding not yet here. It is the append-only, bitemporal
storage the FACT plane wants for history ("what did this rel say at revision N").
Port `labkit/src/sqlite_exp.rs` from rusqlite to sea-orm as `store::temporal`, with
the form-A index-driven close (drive the UPDATE from the delta, never scan the live
index) and the close-only-touched-keys optimization intact. This is prerequisite to
deleting sqlite from labkit (do the port FIRST, then strip — see §5).

**G2 — DRed speed: close the 5x-vs-counting gap.**
DRed pays two full cone passes. Candidate reductions, to measure not assume:
  (a) skip the rederive entirely when the cut node has no alternate-anchored
      descendants (detectable cheaply → falls back to counting's single pass);
  (b) fuse the over-delete + cone-record into one statement per round (drop the
      separate `INSERT cx_cone`);
  (c) semi-naive rederive: seed only from surviving-parent frontier, already done,
      but the inner `JOIN cx_cone` per row is a candidate to precompute.
Target: DRed within ~2x counting (the theoretical two-pass floor), i.e. sub-second
at 960k. The CTE path is a dead end for speed (measured).

**G3 — Wire the CONTROL plane to the FACT plane.**
`reconcile` (dirty/verify) and `cascade` (retract/assert) are both in `RelStore` but
not composed: nothing yet drives a cascade FROM a dirty frontier. The spine wiring
(`cascade.rs` still says "ISOLATED experiment, not wired") is the integration gap.

**G4 — All-pairs vs single-source reachability.**
Everything here is single-source (reach from roots). v5's blast-radius is ALL-PAIRS
transitive closure (`derive.rs` run_reaches_pair). All-pairs is O(V²) output and a
different beast; the lab's `dd_exp.rs` maintains it with `join`. Decide whether the
store maintains all-pairs incrementally or answers it on demand per query.

**G6 — Streaming graph generator (harness honesty).**
`benchgraph::gen_multi` holds the whole graph resident (`Vec<Vec<i64>>` + row/edge
Vecs) before inserting. At extreme scale (5.76M nodes @ 700 MB gun) this BUILDER
staging hits the memcap for every engine at the same size, masking dd's true
resident wall (the store's retract itself is 0.09 MB on disk — see the matrix
`rust_live` column). To observe dd aborting while the store survives, generate +
insert in chunks, freeing each chunk, so peak staging is one chunk not the whole
graph. Then the store completes where dd dies, past the current builder ceiling.

**G5 — Which v5 advanced algorithms belong at the sqlite level.**
From the graph-libs research (`i:graph-libs:sqlite-native`, `relational-graph-
patterns`): recursive CTEs reproduce 4–6 of v5's graph functions on real data with
zero deps. The open placements:
  - reaches_point / reaches_pair (transitive closure) → CTE or the cascade; measured
    doable in SQL.
  - **SCC** → the hard one. Not expressible in a single recursive CTE cleanly; the
    research found LAGraph's SCC scales with graph DEPTH (dies on the 1M-deep worst
    case). Candidate: Tarjan in a driver loop over sqlite-resident adjacency, or a
    partitioned approach. Decide tomorrow.
  - dominators, node2vec/embed_graph → recompute-guarded global ops; likely stay
    compute-side, not sqlite, but confirm against the digest-skip pattern.
